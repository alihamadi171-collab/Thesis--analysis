# E7 — Abu Talal (M, 65, urban Amman; K6 = 18)
DEMOGRAPHIC CONTRADICTION: table records "NA" (not working), but he states: "Currently I work — in violation of the law — I drive school students in a private car, morning, noon and evening" (C102), describes a full working day (C104–C120), and calls the school-run "another project" financed by an instalment car (C357–C369). Informal/unlicensed work may explain why the table records NA; flag for researcher (definition of "current work status").
K6 note: item 5 (tired) answered "physically no, mentally half" — recorded as 3; total 18 consistent with table.
Narrative arc: University graduate (engineering/precision machinery training implied, C412, C645); diploma withheld until military service, so worked in Abu Dhabi (bakery, porter, bread delivery, Ministry of Information press monitor 1982–86, truck driver, then general manager of a 2-truck company) (C153–C186). A partner's false report (Gulf War) stopped his return to the UAE (C192). Ran his father's plastics factory (1991–2003), then his own plastics/recycling factory with injection machines; traded with Jordanian merchants monthly until 2007 (C206–C236). Revolution: machines stolen, car lost, sons detained/beaten at checkpoints; fled to Za'atari then Amman (C248–C259, C699).
Jordan: arrived penniless; sons worked for 200 JD; traders who once hosted him "didn't give me a cup of water" (C383, C533–C534). Wife worked in a sewing workshop for 100 JD — "I'd drop her off and sit and cry, because never in my life had my wife or daughter worked" (C300) — strongest statement of gendered role loss among men. Learned tailoring from his son-in-law, built a home sewing business (curtains, mattress covers for Za'atari, fizon, masks, bags), closed after neighbour hostility and a false "terrorist" report (C303–C347). Now drives school students in an unlicensed car (C102, C592–C594).
Exclusion: (1) nationality — iron-factory owner offered 300 JD "because you're Syrian" — "that phrase hurt me deeply" (C430–C431); (2) age — mobile-repair courses for 18–35 (C442–C444, C544), engineer at a machine shop "you're old" (C503), traders "your bones are gold, what would I hire you for, you're older than me" (C300, C506–C508); no call-back after NGO session where he was the oldest (C467, C489–C493); (3) capital and investors' demand for first-month returns (C415–C426); (4) market loss — 2,000 masks cancelled, then resold to organisations through wasta (C529).
Negative/complicating: denies age discrimination in his own self-employed work (C456–C461, C518–C519) and attributes only fine-motor limits (trembling hands) to age (C545, C571, C579–C581).
Dignity: "work gives you your weight, your value" (C270); being refused hospitality leaves you "without weight" (C531–C534); extortion and insult after a minor car accident — "you're a refugee and you come to drive a car?" — forced silence (C597–C602). Poem of being brought low by time; refused migration to Europe to preserve "my lineage and my dignity"; "I never stretched out my hand to people" (C385).
Relief/meaning: machines "dear to my heart" because they made him feel successful (C349–C355); teaching grandchildren and Tawjihi students gives him "psychological comfort" — knowledge "stays and advances" unlike money (C611–C630). Community efficacy is most explicit in this case.
Future: wants to study/teach technical drawing; offered to teach for 10 JD/hour, refused (C645–C659). Generation can rebuild through small projects by specialty (C666–C678) and by guiding war-raised youth, family gatherings, anti-drug guidance (C693–C699); positive about the new Syrian government (C693). Rights: honour the over-55s; social security so they can keep giving — "a car without fuel doesn't run" (C703).
