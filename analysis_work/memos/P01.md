# E1 — Umm Ayman (F, 51, camp, not currently employed; K6 = 13)
Narrative arc: Did not work in Syria (housewife; living in husband's family house where "going out and coming in" was not allowed, C70, C77). Displacement + husband's death made her the household's provider (C84, C88, C91). In Jordan she worked in a series of short NGO contracts (Mercy Corps/Al-Zahra youth activities, Noor Al-Hussein outreach), most recently dismissed a month before the interview (C128) when a site closed for lack of funding (C178).
Key meanings: Work = relief that occupies the mind and stops sad memories (C242–C243); covering the home "from your own effort" brings psychological comfort (C239). Crafts (wool, crochet, sewing) as both skill and emotional release (C57–C62).
Exclusion: Age criterion experienced directly — NRC accredited computer course limited to under-30s (C215, C108); she felt wronged (C217). Ageist mockery from others ("they'll say she has Alzheimer's", C237; "why did they employ her, she should stay at home", C233). Also: sewing course with no machines afterwards (C104). Barriers named: back problem/disc (C197–C202), young child (C204), shrinking NGO funding (C196).
Negative/complicating: She explicitly says her Al-Zahra dismissal was NOT about age but seniority (C114, C211), and that work difficulty now is not about age (C133). Rejects "money instead of work" for herself (C322, C324) while asking cash for those unable to work (C355).
Gender: displacement paradoxically opened paid work to her (C79–C81) — a role gain alongside loss.
Regard: as a widow with poor small children, felt a certain view from her brothers' household (C98); found the question hard to explain (C101).
Strength: children — "if I weaken, the whole family collapses" (C271); faith/istighfar and hope in God (C265).
Future: house for children not possible as Syrians can't own property (C277); dreams of sewing workshop, blocked by lack of money (C282–C290). Sees little role for her generation in rebuilding because refugees have no money (C308–C314).
Rights message: projects/capital for those who can work, cash for those who cannot (C351–C357); describes camp hardship — people taking food from garbage (C364).
Reflexive note: C102 — interviewer states his belief that improved finances improve psychological state "and this is what I want to prove". Must be acknowledged as a potential leading influence; subsequent answers on dignity/relief (C239–C243) should be read with this in mind, although C242–C243 are elaborated in her own terms.
