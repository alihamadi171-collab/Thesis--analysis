# E4 — Abu Hassan (M, 55, camp; K6 = 24, maximum; not currently employed)
K6 note: scale explained as 1–4 (C10); all answers at the top category, recorded as 24.
Narrative arc: Failed the baccalaureate despite (in his account) correct answers; learned years later he could have appealed — "that destroyed me … I felt I was suffocating" (C71). Joined the army "as an escape from reality" (C66); later moved to a scientific research centre where life felt "civilian" and he earned double the usual salary plus food provisions (C85–C93, C259–C261). Came to Jordan in 2013. Savings ran out after 2–3 years; his daughter worked at a school and supported the house until she was dismissed (C96); he then did metalwork (fabricating tank stands, carts) and laid electricity cables on poles for 8 hours a day (C109–C125, C177).
Role shift and humiliation: "you were the one whose pocket paid … now your daughter gives to you — that is qahr in itself" (C100); "since we came to Jordan I have been humiliated" (C108).
Regard: strongest account of money-dependent regard — guest room full when you have money, nobody says hello when you don't (C102–C106, C223, C301); "we are nothing, we are numbers, like prisoners of Sednaya" (C233).
Exploitation: 4 JD/day for skilled metalwork (C125); contractor halved the hourly rate — "if you want to work, work; if not, go" (C214), which he says applies to everyone, not only older workers (C216).
Age exclusion: refused a tools-plus-certificate electricity course because "over 50 is forbidden" (C195–C203) — "at 50 do I no longer eat or drink?" (C205). Also could not accept being ordered about by a 20–25-year-old guard supervisor; the same reason he disliked the army (C190–C193). Argues older workers are more productive because of experience (mechanic and doctor stories, C311–C382) and proposes pairing older experts as trainers with young workers (C341–C352).
Health: cataract in left eye, right eye at 50%, cannot afford surgery (C120–C122, C213); strength halved since COVID (C175–C189).
Distress and isolation: sits alone at home, avoids people, "my psyche is sick… completely dead" (C41–C44); stopped going to the mosque (C51); "life has turned black… talk of hope doesn't work for me" (C253–C254). Treat as self-description; no diagnostic inference, but note the K6 = 24 and the ethical "stop and support" procedure in the proposal.
Negative/complicating: unlike most, he does not draw strength from faith/family in the interview ("I don't know what will bring back what is gone", C250) — a negative case for COPE themes. He also rejects programs now: "no longer suitable for me, the psyche is destroyed" (C244), although he earlier says he needs work to support his family (C234–C235).
Future: return would not restore his old position (C257–C259); generation can rebuild "if they find a suitable job and salary" (C279) — experience is "75% of work" (C311). Rights message: "put the right person in the right place" (C384–C386); Europe vs Jordan comparison on retirement, insurance and dignity (C207–C213).
Reflexive note: C255–C264 interviewer asks him to imagine restored benefits; answers about restored hope (C269–C276) are hypothetical and prompted.
