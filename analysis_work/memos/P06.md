# E6 — Umm Yusuf (F, 56, urban Amman; K6 = 18 on 1–4 scale)
DEMOGRAPHIC CONTRADICTION: The demographic table records "Current work status: NA" for E6. The interview repeatedly states that she currently works daily in a garment company: "from work to home and from home to work daily" (C37), has worked there about eight years after two months' training (C73–C78), "in the company where I work" (C90), company transport (C97), salary 230–240 JD (C225), pedal machine work hurting her knee (C232–C236). The interview evidence therefore suggests E6 was employed at the time of the interview. This conflicts with the table and with the researcher's statement that only one participant (E8) is employed. Not altered — must be checked by the researcher (possibly "current work" was defined differently, e.g. own project/formal employment).
Narrative arc: From Houran (Daraa); in Syria women "didn't know work"; it was shameful for women to work (C52–C54). After separation from her husband in Jordan she became both "father and mother" for nine years (C57). An NGO course led to placement in a garment factory (C194); she mastered the overlock machine (C85). Before that, home-made makdous/pickles for sale (C209).
Regard: her family in Syria and her own sons object to her working ("why do you work? go back to Syria", "don't work") (C65, C69–C71) — regard shaped by gender norms rather than money.
Age exclusion: since two years, hiring "only up to 35" which she attributes to a Ministry of Labour decision (C90–C92; her understanding, not verified); refused by a clothes-bank training for being over 35 (C103–C107). Yet older women in her factory "work more than the young girls" (C90).
Dignity — key negative/complicating case: initially and firmly says money does not make dignity: "the person who behaves with dignity makes dignity for themselves" (C140–C145). Money does not equal psychological comfort because both those with and without money worry (C121–C151). Under interviewer pressure (C152–C155: "convince me… some people told me it affects") she partly concedes (C156) but then re-separates dignity from money (C157) and locates humiliation in conditional help: "someone gives you money but says go do this, go do that" (C159). Steady income gives relief; insecure work keeps you anxious (C184–C191). This is a methodologically important passage: the participant's own position is that dignity is intrinsic; the researcher's prompting is recorded with REFLEX.
Work preference: a job with guaranteed salary and transport is better than a home project (C214–C216), though the salary (230–240 JD) "does nothing" without support (C225–C226).
Distress: tied to bodily pain (knee) and to being unable to help her sons marry (C232–C241).
Strength: faith — "God closes one door and opens a hundred" (C243–C248).
Future: wishes to return to staying at home (C251) — the only participant who frames rest, not work, as the aspiration; rights message asks for a calm, secure life, housing, and rest for older people rather than being pushed to work and insulted (C293–C298). Negative case for the dominant "projects not cash" message.
Rebuilding: economic reconstruction is for the youth; elders can encourage and give advice (C254–C259). Doubts elders' social role because "there is no respect for the elder" now; young supervisors shout at older women at work (C277–C285).
Gender: criticises men who sit at home while forcing wives to work; women forced to work sometimes beaten (C302–C313).
