# E5 — Umm Bakri (F, urban Amman; table age 54, self-reported 55 at C260; K6 = 14 on 1–4 scale)
Demographic note: table 54 vs self-report 55 (interviewer also says 55, C285). Minor discrepancy; table not changed. Both within 50–65.
Narrative arc: From Aleppo city; in Syria a housewife in comfortable circumstances — family gatherings, outings, "everything was provided" (C57, C63–C68, C208). Lived first in Karak, now Amman. Her husband became ill (C72, C98), so she took on income generation with her children: farm/nursery work with a Danish project (~30 days), a tahini factory (closed), a production-kitchen restaurant that underpaid and finally cheated her (C98–C101), then home-based catering (kibbeh, vine leaves, trays) for clients who came by car (C94, C104). Since moving to Amman she has few clients ("once a month or not at all"), because nobody knows her (C108–C109).
Market: customers in Amman refuse a labour charge of 6–7 JD when others charge 5 — price competition (C125–C130). Program/organisation exclusion by age: associations take 17/20–30 or "50 and under — you're older, you're out" (C143–C145, C294); employers "take the younger ones" (C135–C137).
Counter-narrative on age (very strong): "age, I see it as just a number" (C148); produced more than the young women at the tahini factory, as the manager saw on camera (C146); "I want to be valued like a woman of 30… not stuck in the corner of the house" (C229); "since we came here, you made us old — we still feel young" (C242). She thus simultaneously reports age exclusion (BAR-AGE) and rejects its premise (NEG-AGE) — the contradiction lies between institutional ageism and her self-image.
Role: from housewife to co-provider; "now everyone for himself… we don't know people here" (C75) — social shrinkage after displacement and husband's illness. Regard: "if you have [money] you're worth something" (C73, C77).
Dignity: spending "from your own sweat and brow" is worth more than charity money; earned money feels precious (C196–C198).
Strength: thinking and pressure of obligations — "who will pay the rent? I will" (C210); children and self ("I give myself hope… I feel I'm queen of the whole house", C215–C218). Hope not lost (C20).
Distress: thinking about her children's deprived childhood vs her own; took her 10-year-old son out of school to work (C208).
Future/rebuilding: generation can open projects and rebuild; social rebuilding through pooled family effort ("one hand can't carry a watermelon", C267, C278); the mind works even when the body is ill (C269).
Rights: open projects for older people, not cash (C290–C296).
Reflexive note: interviewer offered the Germany/elderly rebuilding example (C271–C275), acknowledging "I'm not trying to steer"; her C276–C278 answer is in her own terms (pooled house rebuilding).
