# E2 — Abu Muhammad (M, camp; demographic table: 50; K6 = 21, the highest frequency pattern in the sample alongside E4)
Demographic note: The table lists age 50, but in the interview he says he is 49 and "will reach 50" (C8–C10). The interviewer included him on that basis (C22–C23). Flag for researcher: strictly, he was just below the proposal's 50–65 criterion at the time of interview. The table was not changed.
Narrative arc: In Syria (Daraa), ran a high-end clothing shop for a company for ~8–9 years; the shop burned in a strike in 2012 (C108). That work gave him contacts across governorates and a sense of "owning the world" (C121–C129). In Za'tari camp: stall selling cigarettes/phone credit, then currency exchange for ~2 years until camp security banned it as unlicensed (C158, C162); then a 3-year UNICEF sanitation monitoring job (C166); now only occasional 3-month guard rotations "every two years" and otherwise idle (C168, C222).
Exclusion: Primarily attributes exclusion to wasta (connections) and unfair UNHCR-linked selection (C220, C226, C472), and to the cost of work permits outside the camp (C236). Explicitly denies age-based exclusion at first (C246–C258; older men aged 60+ are still hired, C224) — then later recalls being refused a computer course at ~40 because of an under-35 limit, which left him "crushed" (C288–C301, C306). This internal contradiction is analytically important: age-based exclusion may be normalised or not recognised as such until a concrete episode is recalled.
Health: heart patient (C91); hides his illness on registration forms to get a job (C228) — a strategy that shows how health and employability interact.
Dignity: strongest dignity passage in the dataset — shame of asking for a loan at his age, "breaks your spirit"; with money "you leave the shop honoured and respected" (C266, C271).
Distress: daily insomnia "because of psychological pressure, insomnia, the cost of living and illness" (C96); distress "all the time" (C335); links his illness to having no house and no income (C339); became emotional (C351).
Regard: role as father unchanged and his word is still heard (C141, C143), but social relations have thinned because of poverty (C151). Mixed case: stable family authority + reduced social circle.
Hope/future: hope to reopen a shop, even in Syria (C348–C350); would accept monitoring if a program gave capital (C325–C329); wants to transfer his trade experience to his sons (C388–C397).
Generation: "moderate" capacity (C448) — through guidance of children, voluntary clean-up work, guest-house (madafa) gatherings and reconciliation between people (C417–C440); limited for the ill (C411).
Rights: justice and non-discrimination in employment (C465–C468); projects that benefit both UNHCR and the person (C479).
Reflexive note: interviewer prompts "your age is the reason?" (C223) and "the jobs are for young people?" (C233) suggest age as an explanation; participant resisted this framing — strengthening credibility of his NEG-AGE statements.
